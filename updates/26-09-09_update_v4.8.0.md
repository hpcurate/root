## What was done

### DO — the two fixes

**The minimal card keeps its bar** (task 2). It had been cut along with
everything else that took height. It is the one part of the card read at a
glance rather than counted, so it now rides the bottom edge of the row as an
inset strip and costs no height — which was the only reason to drop it.

**A finished routine always closes in Todoist** (task 3). The cause was a silent
drop under contention: `tdBusy` is one lock over six Todoist operations, and
`tdAutoPush()` — the close fired by ticking the last item of a routine — began
`if (tdBusy) return`. Five of the six operations are button presses, where
dropping is right because the button is visibly disabled. This one is not a
Todoist control at all: no button, no toast, no way to know a fetch was in
flight. It now takes a ticket in `tdPending`, and all six release through one
`tdRelease()` that drains the queue on a deferred tick.

### DAY — rearranging, and empty time

**Two arrows on every movable row** (task 1). A move swaps the row with its
neighbour and re-times the pair from the earlier one's start, so the pair keeps
the span it already had and **nothing outside it moves**. A whole-day recompute
was the other way to do it, and it would move rows the user never touched,
which is how a timetable stops being trusted. `fixed` rows are anchors: they
neither move nor are moved past, and a move across a row hidden by
`calShowFixed` / `calShowIdle` is refused with the reason.

**`+ gap`** appends an empty hour on the end and asks only for a length; it is
then walked into place with the same arrows. Composing the two beats a second
dialog asking "after which row?" and getting the answer wrong. It produces the
same `idle` row that "leave it free" already makes on a delete.

### The undo pill

The offset title shadow is **gone permanently** from the pill and its icon. It
belongs on a wordmark read across a room, not on a control two words wide that
appears for five seconds; at that size it read as a printing fault. Titles keep
it.

What is left in the pill is two dials: `undoIcon` (hook, circle, arrow,
chevron, none) and `undoText`. They are two rather than one because the label
and the glyph answer different questions — the label says *what* is being put
back, the glyph says the pill is an undo at all. Turning both off would leave a
control with nothing in it, so the words come back whenever the mark is off.

### TOOLS, rebuilt

Four instruments to two.

- **Removed:** the stopwatch and the countdown, which were the phone's own two
  clocks with a worse readout, and the decider, which answered a question by not
  answering it. `tools.timers` and `tools.decks` leave Config with them; a `v:1`
  store drops those three branches and keeps `pom.days`, which LOG's note reads.
- **The pomodoro** is a control rather than a paragraph: one 74px round button
  that empties out while running, the round as **dots** instead of "round 2 of
  4", skip and reset as small icons off to the side where they cannot be hit
  mid-phase, and a wash of the phase's colour behind the ring while it runs.
- **The Wim Hof round** is new. Three phases: the breaths, at a pace the ring
  breathes to; the retention, counting up and ended by the person; the recovery
  hold. A finished session is written into **LOG's day** as a block and onto
  **DAY's schedule** as a mark, through `LOG.setBlock` and `CAL.markDone` —
  the calls both apps already offer to anything that finishes something. Neither
  app grows a feature for it, and TOOLS records less rather than breaking if one
  is switched off.
- `tools.wimhof` is the one content editor: rounds, breaths, pace, recovery,
  the announcement switch, and the name the session is filed under. A switch
  inside a content editor is new plumbing — one branch in the delegated
  listener that flips it and asks the group to re-read, so there is still no
  per-field state.
