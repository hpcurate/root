## What was done

### The shell: an overlay owns the page until it closes

The four overlay bullets were one rule, as the request guessed. All of it is in
one new section of `js/shell.js`, so it reaches every app rather than the one it
was reported on.

- **The tap that closes the pad is spent.** The backdrop stopped taking pointer
  events the instant its class came off, so the rest of the same gesture landed
  on the form underneath — one tap doing two things. The closing `pointerdown`
  is now `preventDefault`ed *and* `stopPropagation`ed, and the click it would
  have synthesised is swallowed by a document-level capture listener.
- **`padOpen` blurs whatever is focused before the pad is drawn.** This is the
  misalignment. A live system keyboard has already shrunk the visual viewport,
  and a `position:fixed` pad measured against a viewport that has moved has its
  keys somewhere other than where they look. 2.22 hit this by focusing the pad's
  own field and fixed it by not focusing it — but a text field answered *earlier
  on the same form* was still focused and its keyboard was still up, which is
  the path that was left open.
- **A tap outside a focused field blurs it, on every screen.** This is what
  takes the platform's own selection callout off the screen; it has no
  dismissal of its own, which is why it sat stranded over the cold-shower field
  in the screenshot while the caret was in WEIGHT. Blurring is the only thing
  that closes it and nothing was blurring.
- **A gesture that travelled is not a press.** More than 12px from where the
  pointer landed — or a touch the browser has already claimed for a scroll,
  which stops sending `pointermove` and is caught on `touchmove` — marks the
  gesture, and the click at the end never reaches the page. 2.22.3 had taken the
  press *wash* off a scrolled row; the press itself still fired.

A review pass found one hole in this and closed it: a click with no pointer
behind it (Enter on a focused button, or a module calling `.click()`) must not
inherit the last finger's verdict, so a key press ends the open gesture and each
swallow is consumed whether or not it fires.

### The pad says what the number is in

Cold shower reads `45 s`, weight `64.5 kg`, the walk `4 km`, total lifted `kg`,
duration `min`. Declared per field with `data-unit` and **never inferred** — a
unit guessed from a label is a unit that is wrong on the one field nobody
checked. `duration` and `clock` ignore it, because 7h20m and 09:30 are already
units.

**Decision made on your behalf.** The request said "like it does for wakeup
time with the seconds", and asked where the units should go. You picked the
numpad display over a suffix in the field. Cold shower's seconds appear nowhere
in the app outside that input, and a pad-owned field is never focused — so while
you answer it, the pad's own label and the number are the only things on screen,
and the label the pad covers is often the only place the unit was written down.
Nothing goes near the `.md`.

### LOG: discard undoes, and "written" means someone wrote it

- **Discard.** The forms do not hold their answers in the DOM: the scales, the
  meds, the meals, the counters, the cold-shower toggle and the blocks write
  straight into the live record as they are tapped, and `save()` only copies the
  text fields across and flushes the object. "Go back without saving" left every
  one of those edits in the record, and the next write from *anywhere* committed
  them — an entry, a block ticked on DO (`setBlock` persists the same object),
  the other half of the day. That is exactly "I went back without saving and it
  saved anyway", delayed until something else touched the day. `discard()`
  re-reads the day from storage, which also keeps whatever was legitimately
  written while the form was open.
- **Written.** `saveMorning` / `saveEvening` leave an `m.saved` / `e.saved`
  stamp, which is authoritative. Records from before it fall back to the old
  field scan with `blocks` taken out of the evening's — `blocks` is the one
  field the app writes on its own, and it was what turned the card green,
  cleared the `!` and extended the streak for an evening nobody had filled in.
  This drives `morningLogged` / `eveningLogged`, so the card, the month grid,
  the streak and the tab's `!` all follow from one place, as before.
- **The month grid.** The selected day is inverted (ink and ground swapped)
  rather than ringed, and every day number is bold.

### DO

- **A block-labelled task is the blocks section's, not also a today row.** The
  two are separate questions asked of Todoist and a task due today carrying
  `@b1` answered both, so it was drawn twice and counted twice in the tab badge.
  Dropped at *draw* time rather than fetch time, so switching the blocks section
  off hands it straight back with no refetch.
- **How far back "show done" reaches is a setting** — day (exactly the old
  behaviour, and the default), week or month, on DO's own panel. DO's cache is
  one day deep and always was; the earlier days come from LOG, where the tick
  already files the name (`LOG.blocksBefore`). They are drawn as names under a
  date rather than as tiles, because a tile offers a tick that cannot exist —
  reopening a block finished last Tuesday is Todoist's job.

### DAY

- **A line at the hour it is**, on today only, and only while today is still
  running. Summed from the same durations the rows are drawn from rather than
  measured off the DOM, so it lands on the boundary it names whatever `calHour`
  is set to. It rides the shell's existing minute tick and moves the line alone.
- **Every `task` and `fixed` row can be ticked off.** An `idle` row cannot — it
  is the absence of an event. A tick is a mark on the drawing and nothing else:
  CAL has no network by contract (ROOT.md §8) and a stored event carries a
  resolved name, not a task id.
- **The day's slots can be filled from the blocks DO is holding.** One row per
  block, the day's own slots beside it, PLAN's slot rules (a slot another task
  holds is refused *by name*; tapping the one a task holds gives that hour
  back). It rewrites every slot, so the ones left empty go back to `idle`, and
  it asks before it does.

**Two decisions made on your behalf here, both worth knowing about:**

1. **It works on a day that already exists, not on an empty one.** The slots
   come from the stored record; CAL does not resolve `plan.dayTemplates` and
   ROOT.md §9 is explicit that it must not — a day drawn from a template that
   was never sent anywhere is a day this app is not allowed to claim. On an
   empty day the existing "open plan" action is still the answer. Your bullet
   said "overwrites blocks on current day schedule", which reads as a day that
   has one.
2. **The day is marked `localEdit` — "edited here" in the head, permanently.**
   Everywhere else on DAY, what is drawn is what PLAN resolved and handed to
   Todoist. This breaks that for one day, and a drawing that quietly claims to
   be the plan is the one thing DAY must not be. Nothing is sent: the tasks stay
   as they are in Todoist and the calendar keeps whatever PLAN already told it,
   and the confirmation says so.

The confirmation uses `Shell.confirm`, which honours the "confirm before
clearing" setting — the same dialog "clear this day" already uses, and a lighter
action than that one. If you switch that setting off you will get no prompt
here, which is the setting doing what it says.

What the new ones actually pin down:

- The pad unit appearing per field and only where declared; a duration left
  alone; nothing inferred from a label.
- The three overlay rules present in the shell rather than in one app, and the
  keyboard-click hole closed.
- Discard leaving the record untouched, a later write from elsewhere unable to
  resurrect the discarded edits, and cancel keeping you on the form.
- A blocks-only evening reading as unwritten, saving the form stamping it, and
  the blocks themselves untouched by either.
- The now line landing inside the hour it names and absent on a day that is not
  today; a task row and a template row tickable and an idle row not; the tick
  persisting across leaving the day and coming back.
- The schedule panel listing DO's blocks against the day's own slots, refusing a
  taken slot by name, giving an hour back, asking before it overwrites,
  rewriting every slot, leaving the template's hours alone, marking the day
  edited — and `js/cal.js` still containing no URL, no `fetch(` and no
  `XMLHttpRequest`.
- A block-labelled task kept out of the today list and handed straight back when
  the blocks section is switched off.
- The done window at day / week / month, reaching into LOG's records, drawn as
  names and not as buttons, and persisted.
- The month grid's selected day inverted, declared after the tints so it beats
  them, and the numbers bold.

**Not verified: how any of it looks, or feels under a finger.** The Chrome
extension is not connected, so there was no browser pass at all. The touch
behaviours in particular — the swallowed tap, the blur, the 12px slop, the pad
drawn over a dismissed keyboard — are argued from the platform's semantics and
held in place by structural checks, not driven under a thumb. They are the part
of this release most worth trying on the phone before trusting.

Two review passes on my own diff found and fixed three things the harness would
not have: a click with no pointer behind it inheriting a stale gesture verdict, a
full redraw of an off-screen slide once a minute, and the schedule panel being
unreachable on a day whose rows are all switched off in settings.
