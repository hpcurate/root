## What was done

### The blur — the status bar was never ours (task 1)

`apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style: black-translucent` asks iOS to hand the
page the whole screen, status bar included, and to draw its own material over
that strip. Everything the band puts up there is therefore **underneath
something the app does not control and cannot opt out of from inside the page**.
That is exactly where the title band's top row sits, and it is why the row read
as blurred while identical type twenty pixels lower did not.

Three releases went looking for the cause in CSS:

| Release | Found | Real? | It? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2.24.0 | a fractional wordmark → `--title-px` | yes | no |
| 2.24.1 | a fractional status-bar inset → `--sat` | yes | no |
| 2.25.0 | `-webkit-overflow-scrolling:touch` on the scroller under the band | yes | no |

All three are kept. Each was a genuine defect and each is worth having fixed on
its own terms. But four reports of an unchanged screen, against three
independent and correct CSS-side fixes, is enough evidence that the thing
drawing over the band was never in the stylesheets.

`black` gives the strip back to iOS. It reserves the bar, paints it black, and
`env(safe-area-inset-top)` becomes 0 — so the app starts below it and nothing of
ours sits under the system's material.

**The cost, stated plainly:** ROOT no longer runs edge-to-edge at the top. On
the dark presets, whose ground is `#0e0e0e`, that is close to invisible; on a
light preset it is a black bar above a pale app. **Reverting is one word in one
meta tag** — everything else (the band, the six sticky headers, `--sat`) is
written against `env()` and follows either value without a change. A harness
check asserts that, so the revert stays a one-word revert.

**Decision made on Hugo's behalf:** taking this at all. It was offered in the
2.25.0 task comment with the trade-off named, and "blur is still here" was the
reply. `viewport-fit=cover` stays, so the bottom inset for the home indicator is
unaffected.

### STORE — the total cannot leave the band (task 2)

2.25 put the item count and the total at the same end of the same row. Two big
things at one end of a phone-wide band is how the total ran off the edge.

- **The count moved to the date line** — `SAT 5 SEP · 3/11 ITEMS`, the shape DO
  uses for "9 TO DO". That is task 2's "change the way the item count displays",
  and it is also the fix for the overflow: the count is small, the date line has
  room for it, and the wordmark's row no longer has to find any.
- **The total has that row to itself**, sized at `min(--title-px × .6, 10.5vw)`
  — the wordmark's size scaled down *and* capped against the viewport, so a
  four-figure basket at the largest title size still fits beside `STORE.`.
  `min-width:0`, `overflow:hidden` and the band's own `overflow:hidden` are the
  backstop if it ever still does not.

### STORE — the total's face (task 3 + the session)

**White.** It is the loudest number in the app while it is up, and the pinned
widget below already speaks in the accent; two accents arguing at the top of the
screen is worse than one plain number.

**The shadow** is a `text-shadow` with **zero blur**, `.055em` to the right and
no vertical offset — a second copy of the glyphs, displaced, sitting under them.
That is task 3 read literally: "same shape as the text just slightly right,
under the text layer". Not a soft drop shadow, which would be depth rather than
a copy. The currency mark opts out of it, or the `€` would double into mush at
0.4em.

**Decision made on Hugo's behalf:** the shadow's colour. The task said to ask,
and the session set the *text* to white but not the shadow. On a near-black
ground a dark shadow is invisible, so it has to be a colour; it takes the
accent, which is also what stops a white number looking like it came from the
operating system rather than from this app. One word redirects it.

**The change.** Green as it rises, red as it falls, easing back to white when it
settles. Three things make it smooth rather than flashy:

- The colour is the whole signal, so the movement under it is a **nudge** —
  `.055em` up on a rise, down on a fall. Enough to catch the direction, not
  enough for the number to appear to jump.
- The class is dropped after ~620ms and the colour returns through a
  `--dur-3` transition, so it **eases** back to white rather than snapping.
- Restarted, not queued. Keying a price in a digit at a time fires this several
  times a second, and each run must replace the last.

The shadow takes the colour with it, so the two never disagree about what is
happening.

**Arriving is deliberately not a change.** Pinning the counter mid-trip mounts
the number (a short slide in from the right, like the day number) rather than
flashing it green — "you just spent forty euros" is not true of money that was
already spent. `costWas` is `null` until something has actually been shown, and
unpinning resets it.

## Verification

`test/harness.mjs` — **693 checks, all green** (679 at the end of 2.25), plus
braces balanced across twelve stylesheets, every touched module parsed, and the
site served over http.

New coverage: the meta tag changed and `black-translucent` gone; the bottom
inset still claimed; every header still measuring from `env()`, so the revert
works without touching anything else; all three earlier fixes still in place;
the count on the date line with the total alone on the wordmark row, and
`.h-band-end` gone; the total sized against the viewport as well as the title;
the shadow being hard, offset, and not doubled on the currency mark; mounting
not flashing; green on a rise and red on a fall with the shadow following; the
nudge being a nudge; the ease back to white; and a repaint that changes nothing
not re-firing.

Three checks from 2.25 were **rewritten rather than deleted** — they asserted
the shared `.h-band-end` this release replaces, and what they were guarding
(the cost mounting only when pinned, the two being distinguishable, the widget
dropping the price) is still checked against the new layout.

## Deferred

**Task 1 stays open in Todoist for a fourth release**, and for the last time in
this shape. jsdom cannot render a blur and the browser extension would not
connect, so the only person who can close it is Hugo looking at the screen.

What is different from the three previous attempts is that this is not another
guess at a CSS defect. It is the one candidate that was always outside CSS, and
the change is not a workaround — the app has simply stopped asking iOS to draw
over it.

If it survives even this, the remaining candidate is `html{zoom:var(--ui-scale)}`
at a non-default Interface scale, which would be confirmed or ruled out in one
message by saying what that dial is set to.

Nothing else in the batch was left out.
