## What was done

### TOOLS — the strip is four instruments

- **Renamed the Wim Hof round to `breathing`** in the strip, in the settings
  section and in the Config default that names a finished session. The Config
  *path* stays `tools.wimhof`: ROOT.md §5 makes storage keys a contract, and a
  word is not a reason to migrate one. Decided on Hugo's behalf, and called out
  here because it is the kind of thing that looks like an oversight later.
- **On-tool dials, folded behind one word.** The pomodoro carries focus, break
  and rounds; breathing carries rounds, breath and recovery, at exactly the
  ranges the brief gave (1–5, 1–5 by 0.25, 5–30s). A dial reads the settings
  default until it is moved, and the readout says `default` while it does. The
  ranges live in one table (`DIAL` in `js/tools.js`) that both the tool and the
  settings editor read, so a default cannot be set to a number the tool would
  clamp the moment you touched it. The default `pace` moved 2.2 → 2.25 to sit on
  the 0.25 grid the brief asked for.
- **The big button is square,** with `toolsBtnSize` and `toolsBtnRound` in
  appearance → the tools panel. The corner is a **percentage of the button's own
  size**, so 50% is a circle at any size and the two dials never have to be set
  in step with each other.
- **`toolsShown`** is the strip: which instruments it carries and in what order,
  edited as a row per instrument with a switch and two arrows. It is the same
  shape `apps` has — the list *is* the visibility, so an instrument switched off
  keeps its place for when it comes back on. One has to stay.
- **A history on each instrument's own page.** One row a day, lines and nothing
  else: for the pomodoro the day's rounds as bars and its total; for breathing
  every hold in the day. The accent marks the one thing worth *finding* — the
  best day, the best hold ever posted — and nothing else, because three
  highlights are no highlight.
- **Everything that finishes is written down.** A completed focus round now goes
  to LOG's day and DAY's schedule through the same two calls a session already
  used (`writeDown()`), and so does a finished run. The pomodoro gained a
  `label` in Config for what to call it, defaulting to `focus`.

### TOOLS — optimise

- A **shelf** of lists, one row each: icon, name, step count, best time, START.
  The best time is *on* the row because it is the reason to press the button.
- A **run** is the clock, the step you are on, and two buttons: one for the
  clock, one for the step. The clock runs through a step change — a route has no
  gap between its steps, and stopping it to record one would measure the
  recording.
- **Splits** land as they are taken, with the difference against that list's best
  for that step, and a split that beats it comes back **gold**: a warm gradient
  clipped to the type with a bloom under the row, rather than another colour
  state. The comparison is the instrument; gold is the comparison made visible.
- A **history** with the filters the brief listed: recent / fastest / by list,
  records only, and a per-list show-hide.
- **Lists are Config** (`tools.optimise`), edited as a card each: name, colour
  (a palette, a colour of its own, the accent, or an accent gradient — all four
  the brief asked for), one of **twenty** icons drawn inline by TOOLS itself, and
  the steps one a line. The editor asks TOOLS for its icons and its palette
  rather than keeping a second copy of either.
- Times are filed under a list's **id**, minted once and never re-derived from
  its name, so renaming a list keeps its record.
- On the type: the list's **name** is the thing you are looking for on a shelf
  row, so it is the one set in the title face at 15px; the times under it are
  read second and stay in the mono. On a split, the **time** is the answer and
  carries the title face; the step's name does not.

### TOOLS — data

Five readings of what the other three wrote down, all inline SVG against the
same tokens as everything else — no library and no canvas:

- a **tile** per series with its total and its per-day average;
- the **range day by day** as columns, so a day with nothing in it reads as a
  gap rather than a line drawn through it;
- a **pie** of the share each instrument took, drawn only when there is more
  than one slice;
- a **nightingale wheel** of the week — each weekday a wedge whose radius is how
  much that day usually holds, each series normalised to its own peak first so
  minutes do not drown a count of three;
- a **kept-up strip**: how many of the last days had anything on them at all,
  and the run that is still going.

Filters for the range (week / month / year) and for which instrument, both
remembered.

### CREATE — practice

- The shelf opens on **one counter per area**: the total in the title face, the
  area's name in its own colour, the session count, and a rail showing how it
  compares with the busiest area. Under each, `+30` and `+60`.
- The window is **a word in the section's own title line** and cycles on a tap,
  as asked — three answers in a fixed order, so tapping through is the whole
  control and a chip row would compete with the sort chips forty pixels below.
- **Several taps in a minute are one session.** A practice session carries `at`,
  the wall clock of its last tap; a tap inside `GROUP_MS` of it adds to that
  session instead of starting one. This is the rule the whole thing turns on — a
  log that recorded four sessions for one hour would make the session count
  worthless.
- The section **follows the strip**: `all` shows every counter, an area shows its
  own. Which areas get one at all is a switch per area in CREATE's settings,
  drawn from the areas themselves so a renamed or new area is there without
  anything else changing. Switching one off keeps its hours — the list says which
  areas get a *counter*, never which sessions exist.
- A practice session is an ordinary session with no work and a `practice` flag,
  so it reaches the week under the shelf, LOG's note and both reports like every
  other hour at the desk. Logging one repaints the whole shelf rather than only
  the counter — two numbers on one page disagreeing about the same afternoon is
  worse than either of them being wrong.

### DAY — edit mode

- **One switch** in the day's action row. Off, a row is a name, a clock and —
  where it can be ticked — a tick, and every reshaping control is simply absent.
  On, the delete, the move arrows and two new length buttons appear and the day
  says so. Nothing is restyled: what edit mode changes is what a row *carries*.
- **Longer and shorter**, by `calStep` (15 minutes by default, 5–60 in CAL's
  settings). The row changes and everything after it moves by the same amount —
  the ordinary thing a calendar does — stopping at the first `fixed` row, which
  is an anchor and does not move. Growing into one is refused and says which row
  is in the way, the same rule the arrows already followed. A row cannot be
  shrunk below one step: a row of nothing is a row that is not there, and
  deleting is what that is for.
- Edit mode is deliberately **not** a preference: it is where you are, not how
  you like things, and a day left in it a week ago should not still be in it.

### The band's wordmark

- The shell puts the app's **own tab glyph** inside every `.h-logo` at boot —
  inside rather than beside it, because five of the eleven bands have no row
  around their title and a sibling would have needed two layouts. Settings takes
  `#tab-set`, the short name its tab button uses.
- `bandMark` in appearance → density: **the name**, **icon and name**, or **the
  icon**. Icon-only collapses the type rather than hiding the wordmark (the icon
  is inside it) and draws the mark at full title size to hold the weight the word
  was holding.
- It is a title, so it is treated as one: sized off `--title-px` and carrying the
  same hard offset shadow every other title has, as a `drop-shadow` because the
  mark is a drawing.
