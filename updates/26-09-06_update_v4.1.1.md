## What was done

### CURATE is a project, not a label

`js/config.js`, `js/create.js`, `js/search.js`, `index.html`. `create.curate` is
now `{ project, maxAgeMin }`. The fetch resolves the project by **folded** name
— the way PLAN resolves every project it sends to, so `02 | curate` and
`02curate` are the same one and a rename that only moves the punctuation does
not break the tab — then reads its tasks and its sections.

Sorted the way it is arranged over there:

- **section order** first, with the project's unsectioned tasks in a group that
  sorts above the sections, because that is where Todoist itself puts them
- then each task's **own order** inside its section
- **subtasks nested** under the task they belong to, in their own order. They
  are tasks of the same project and arrive in the same answer, so they are filed
  under their parent instead of beside it. A subtask whose parent is closed, or
  lives elsewhere, is drawn as a task of its own rather than dropped — the
  alternative is a row that silently is not there.
- a section with nothing open in it is not drawn at all: an empty heading is a
  row of furniture saying "nothing here"

A row also carries its due date when it has one. The chip on the strip wears
what is after the last pipe — `curate`, not `02 | curate` — since the number and
the pipe are how it sorts in a sidebar and neither is worth a chip's width.

Still **read-only**, and the two harness checks that hold that line are
unchanged.

### The glider slides — and why it did not

`js/create.js`, `index.html`. **This was a real bug, not a tuning problem.** 4.1
rewrote `.cr-areas`'s `innerHTML` on every selection, so the glider was a
brand-new element already at its destination: it had nothing to transition
*from*. The one element whose entire job is to move did not move, and nothing
failed anywhere — the class was right, the position was right, the transition
was declared.

Both strips keep a `data-sig` of their chips now and only rewrite the markup
when *that* changes (an area renamed, added or deleted in Settings). A selection
moves the `.active` class and asks the glider to travel. DO's `setTab` has always
worked this way; this is the same fix arrived at from the other side, and
ROOT.md §6 carries the general form: anything animated across a re-render has to
check whether the node survived.

The session log's filter had the same problem and a second cause — it was
written inside the body it filters, so every tap rebuilt both. It has its own
box now (`#cr-log-tabs`), outside `#cr-sessions`.

### The chips are even, and centred

`css/create.css`. `flex:1 1 0` gives every chip the same share of the rail
whatever its label is long, and `min-width:max-content` is the floor that stops
the share squashing a word — so four chips split the width when there is width
to split, and the rail scrolls when there is not. Sized to their own text they
were four different widths with the words hard against the left of each, which
is what "not separating every item evenly and the items are not centered" was.

**Decision made on Hugo's behalf:** DO's copy of this strip is deliberately left
alone. It shares the title band with a wordmark and has no width of its own to
share out, so evenly-split chips there would either crowd the wordmark or leave
the strip looking padded. That is now one of the two places the two strips are
allowed to differ, and §6 names both rather than leaving the divergence to be
rediscovered.

### The count moved into the band

`index.html`, `css/create.css`, `js/create.js`, `js/shell.js`. The 74px hero is
gone — it was most of a phone screen spent on one digit, on the screen whose job
is to list what is on the shelf.

The count sits at the right end of the wordmark's row now, in the same
`.h-daynum` box LOG's and DAY's big day numbers live in: same size, same
`--title-cap` box, same offset shadow, and **the same shuffle**. The label above
the wordmark says what it is counting — `in progress`, `mixing · in progress`,
or the curate project's name.

`Shell.dayNum` was the only thing in ROOT that could play that roll and it only
understood dates. It is **`Shell.rollNum(box, text, sort)`** now — the same
function, with the ISO string generalised into a `sort` key that says which way
to throw the outgoing digit — and `dayNum` is one line on top of it. Three
boxes, one function, and the per-box state and cleanup timers that 3.0 needed
are untouched. CREATE passes the count as its own sort key, so a number going up
rolls one way and down rolls the other.

The total the hero also carried is not lost: the "Finished" fold below says how
many are done, which is the same subtraction.

**The band is redrawn on every screen**, not only the shelf. It belongs to the
slide — the shell lifts it out of `#s-home` at boot — and moving a work to its
finished stage happens on the *work* screen and changes the number.

### Progress is ticks, not a bar

`js/create.js`, `css/create.css`. It was a full-width 4px rail with `1 / 7`
printed beside it, on a line of its own: three shapes saying one thing, and the
widest of them carrying the least. A rail can only say *what fraction* — the
number next to it existed because the fraction was not enough.

One segment per checklist item says both at once and needs no number: how far
along, and out of how many. It is also the shape the stage strip at the top of
the shelf already uses, so the screen has one way of drawing "some of these, not
all of them" instead of two. The count survives for a screen reader
(`aria-label="1 of 7 done"`), which is what the ratio was actually for.

Past sixteen items the segments would be thinner than the gaps between them, so
it falls back to a rail — a checklist that long is a fraction again.

The week's split bars went to the same 5px, so the two bars on that screen read
as one weight rather than two. They stay continuous, because a share of the week
is a proportion and not a count of anything.
