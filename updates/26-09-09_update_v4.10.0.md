## What was done

### The cursor moved through the markup, not through the screen — task 1

This is the third pass at keyboard navigation and the first two shared one bug,
which is why it kept feeling wrong rather than feeling nearly right.

Both walked the focusable list in **document order**. DO's home is a two-column
grid, so the next node in the document is the card to the *right*, and the one
after that is down-and-left. **Pressing down moved right.** Every grid, every
row of chips and every pair of buttons on a row did the same thing. "Scrolls
between elements inconsistently" was a precise description of exactly that.

A direction is now a direction. `nearest(dir)` picks the closest candidate that
genuinely lies that way, measured off the rectangles the browser has already
laid out — the same rule the eye is using, which is why it needs no per-screen
knowledge to feel right. Something sharing a row or a column with the cursor is
almost always the thing meant, so being out of line costs eight times what
distance does.

Up and down fall back to document order when nothing lies that way, so two keys
still reach everything on a screen. Left and right deliberately do not: a *left*
that jumps to the end of the previous row is the surprise this was built to
remove.

**All four arrows are the cursor now.** Two of them used to change tab, which
meant the four keys shaped like a direction pad did two unrelated jobs, and
left/right could not be used for what they obviously mean. Tabs are the letters
and 1–9.

### One hand

"Let me use the app with one hand" is a question about *where the keys are*, and
it cannot be answered from here — the defaults are already a guess about one
keyboard layout and this install may not be on it. Shipping a second guess would
have been the same mistake twice.

So it asks. **Set them** takes the four directions and the use key in order,
binding each as it is pressed: put a hand down where it is comfortable, press
five keys, done. Whatever is reachable is what gets bound, on any layout.

### The highlight

4.5 drew a hard 2px accent ring and it was too loud. 4.9 drew a hairline wash
and it was too quiet. That is not a volume problem, so this is a choice of kind
rather than a slider — `kbMark`, four treatments:

- **lift** (default) — outlined, haloed and scaled a touch, so it reads as
  raised off the page rather than as a box drawn around it.
- **corner arrow** — 4.9's, for when the quietest thing that works is wanted.
- **edge bar** — a thick accent rule down the left edge. The eye finds a
  vertical rule at the margin without scanning for it.
- **spotlight** — everything else goes dark. One `100vmax` shadow does it, which
  is why nothing has to be targeted at the siblings: the selection punches a
  hole in a sheet over the whole screen.

### TOOLS' layouts were set but never drawn — task 2

The dial changed, the chip lit, and the screen carried on drawing the ring —
which from the outside looks exactly like "not selectable in settings".

`tools.js` subscribed to `Config` and not to `Prefs`. Every other app has
subscribed to Prefs for versions; TOOLS had never had a Prefs dial of its own to
subscribe for, until 4.9 gave it one and did not add the line to match. One
line, and a check that now drives the dial and asserts the drawing changed.

## Verification

`node test/update.mjs check` — 20 syntax checks, smoke across 10 apps / 15
themes / 14 panels, **1110 behaviour checks and 6 runner tests passed** (18
added, 2 rewritten where they pinned the arrows to the tabs).

The scoring is checked against a hand-built two-column grid — four rectangles
laid out the way DO's cards are — and asserts that *down* goes to the card
underneath rather than the one beside it, that *right* goes beside, that *up*
comes back up its own column, and that a direction with nothing that way picks
nothing. That is pure arithmetic on rectangles, so it is provable here even
though jsdom lays nothing out, and it is precisely the part that was wrong.

The TOOLS fix is checked behaviourally: set the dial, assert the drawing
changed, with nothing else prompting a render.
