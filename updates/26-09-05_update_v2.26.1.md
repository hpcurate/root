# v2.26.1 — the shadow was the blur, and the fold was the other one

**Project:** root
**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Bump:** patch (2.26.0 → 2.26.1)
**Commit:** _filled in after pushing_

## Request

**Source:** the session. Hugo, on 2.26.0:

> sub screen title has the shadow effect but not the actual title "DO." "STORE."
> etc. blur is back, changing the interface scale to 100% fixed it back now it's
> just permanently blurry.

Three things, and two of them are 2.26.0's own regressions.

## What was done

### The shadow was a sub-pixel smear

`--title-sh` was `.055em`. Measured out:

| where | size | `.055em` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| sticky sub-screen title | 15px | **0.825px** |
| big date / wordmark | 54px | 2.97px |

At 54px that is a proper offset copy, which is what it was designed against and
what made it look right on STORE's total in 2.25.1. **At 15px it is 0.825px** —
a glyph drawn twice, less than a pixel apart. That is not a shadow, it is the
textbook way to make text look out of focus, and 2.26.0 applied it to every
sticky title in the app.

`max(1px, round(.055em, 1px))`: a whole pixel at every size it is used at — 1px
on a sticky title, 3px on a wordmark — with a plain `1px` first declaration as
the fallback for a browser without `round()`. The `em` stays inside the value
rather than being resolved at `:root`, because a custom property is substituted
at the point of use, which is what lets one token scale per element.

### The fold was the other regression

2.26.0 retired Interface scale for the right reason — document zoom multiplies
every length by a fraction, so whole-pixel type stops being whole-pixel — and
then **folded the stored scale into Spacing.**

`--dens` multiplies every padding and gap in the app: `calc(18px * var(--dens))`.
At 1.1 that 18px padding becomes 19.8px, every box below it starts on a
fractional offset, and the text inside sits on a fractional baseline.

**That is the same defect, moved from one multiplier to another.** And it was
worse than the original, because the control that could be set back to 100% had
just been deleted — which is exactly what "now it's just permanently blurry"
means.

The scale is dropped rather than folded. An install that 2.26.0 folded is
repaired: Spacing returns to its default once, recorded in a new stored flag
`densRepair` so the repair runs a single time and never touches a Spacing chosen
afterwards. The flag is not a setting and has no control — it is a record, like
`appsSeen`.

**Decision made on Hugo's behalf:** resetting Spacing rather than trying to
recover a pre-2.26.0 value. 2.26.0 overwrote it without recording the original,
so there is nothing to recover; anyone who had deliberately set Spacing before
2.26.0 loses it a single time. A soft app is not worth preserving either value.

The Spacing dial now carries a note saying what it does to text at anything but
100%. It has always had this property; nothing had ever said so, and the same
blind spot is what produced the fold.

### The wordmarks wear the shadow

2.26.0 put it on the sticky sub-screen headers and on the big dates, and left
out the one thing in the app most obviously a title: `DO.`, `STORE.`, `LOG.`

Set once in `shell.css` alongside the rest of the band's shared shape, rather
than in eight app stylesheets.

## Verification

`test/harness.mjs` — **713 checks, all green** (710 at 2.26.0), braces balanced,
every touched module parsed, site served over http.

New coverage: the shadow offset rounded with a 1px floor and its fallback
present; the wordmark wearing it; a stored Interface scale dropped rather than
folded into Spacing; the repair recorded so it runs once and not on every boot;
a Spacing chosen *after* the repair left alone; and no control existing for the
repair flag.

Three checks from 2.26.0 were rewritten — they asserted the `.055em` offset and
the fold, which are the two things this release corrects.

## Notes

2.26.0 shipped two regressions in one release, and they share a cause: both came
from applying a value that was verified at one size or one setting and assumed
to hold everywhere. `.055em` was checked against a 54px number and shipped onto
15px titles. The zoom retirement was reasoned correctly about zoom and then
reached for the nearest available multiplier without asking whether *that* one
had the same property — which it did, and which the very same release had just
spent five paragraphs explaining.
