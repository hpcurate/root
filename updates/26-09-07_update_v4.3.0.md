## What was done

### TOOLS — a tenth app (task 4)

Four instruments behind one strip: a **pomodoro**, a **stopwatch**, a
**countdown** and a **decider**.

It is one tab and not four because none of these is an app — each is one
control, one readout and one number kept for the day, and four tabs would be
four tabs empty most of the time. It is CREATE's tab strip again. There are no
sub-screens, deliberately: a place to navigate to is a place to forget you left
something running.

**The clock is a timestamp, never a counter.** Every running thing stores the
wall-clock moment it ends (the stopwatch stores its start plus what was banked)
and every readout is `Date.now()` against that. A `setInterval` that increments
a number is wrong here three times over — a background tab is throttled, a
sleeping phone stops firing it, a reload loses it — and all three are exactly
the case a pomodoro is used in. The interval that exists only paints, runs
whatever tab you are on, and is not created while nothing is running.

Config holds the four pomodoro lengths, the quick-timer chips and the decider's
lists (they are content: 50/10 is a different working day, not a
different-looking one). `tools_v1` holds what is running and the day's tally of
finished focus rounds, capped at 90 days.

New: `js/tools.js`, `css/tools.css`, the slide and the `tab-tools` sprite in
`index.html`, `'tools'` in `Prefs.APPS`, the settings panel and its content box,
`PANELS` / `CATS` / `SEG_NAMES` / `APP_NAMES` / `APP_HINTS` / `RENDERERS` /
`GROUPS` / `EDITOR_ORDER` in `settings.js`, the two editors (`tools.timers`,
`tools.decks`), `APP_NAMES` and one `CONTENT` line in `search.js`, and the
`tools` branch in `config.js`. That is exactly ROOT.md §7's list and nothing
else, which is why §7 now names TOOLS as its worked example.

### Sound — a kit, and a map over it (task 3)

`Prefs.sound()` now takes one of **five moments** instead of three: `tap`,
`nav`, `menu`, `done`, `msg`. Nine voices — `tick` `click` `wood` `pop` `thunk`
`blip` `swell` `chime` `glass`, plus `none` — where the clicky five are square
and triangle waves with a lowpass rolling the top off, which is what makes a
tick a tick and not a beep. `blip` / `swell` / `chime` are the three 4.2 shipped,
kept under their own names.

Five **kits** map the five moments at once: `tick` (the new default), `classic`
(exactly what 4.2 sounded like), `wood`, `glass`, `minimal`. On top of the kit,
five per-event overrides sit on `auto` until one of them is wrong. Every chip in
both controls plays its own sound as it is picked, because "thunk" and "glass"
are not words anyone can choose between by reading.

**The two new moments are decided in `shell.js`, not announced by an app.**
`pressVoice()` reads the element under the finger: a tick about to go *on* is
`done`, one coming back off is a plain `tap`. There are still exactly three call
sites in the shell and zero in every app module, and the harness fails if either
number moves — which is what keeps TOOLS free of a line of audio.

### The top bar, and the bottom pill (tasks 7, 10)

**Content offset** — a 0–24px dial, six by default, folded into `--sat` so it
moves the wordmark band *and* every sticky sub-screen header at once. Whole
pixels only, rounded in `Prefs.apply()` rather than trusted from the store,
because a fraction there lands in every header's padding and the band's
min-height — the exact shape of the bug this exists to stop chasing.

**Decision made on Hugo's behalf:** the request was "just bring all the content
down a few pixels", which could have been a fixed offset. It is a dial instead,
because "a few" is a number that has to be turned until it looks right on the
phone it is being looked at on, and because that is the one thing five earlier
rendering fixes could not do. The default is a few pixels, so the literal
request is what an untouched install gets.

**Bar height** (44–76px), **Bar corners** (0–34px) and **Tab icon size**
(14–26px) are three new dials on the layout panel, defaulting to the literals
they replaced — 58px, 29px (what 999px resolved to) and 19px — so an install
that never touches them looks identical. `--nav-r` is a length rather than
`--r-pill`, so one dial spans a square bar to the pill it ships as, and
`--nav-icon` reaches the two rules that used to override the icon size outright
(the wide breakpoint, labels-off) as ratios of itself.

### PLAN and DAY — patching one block (tasks 5, 6)

`mode: patch` is a third mode in the export contract: write the slot lines and
change nothing else on the day. `blocks` was being read as a whole-day
instruction, and fairly — it carries `start:` and `template:`.

- The header repeats **the stored day's own** `start:` and `template:` rather
  than the form's, so a patch cannot move the day. The four fields stay a
  contract; the only new information is the slot lines.
- It cannot be picked for a day ROOT has no record of. The chip is drawn, dimmed
  and refuses with a word — the same shape a slot the template does not have has.
  Changing the date out from under a picked `patch` drops it to `blocks`, out
  loud.
- `CAL.patch()` is the local half: only the named slots are written into a day
  that already exists, and nothing else is touched. A patched row keeps its own
  stored hours. It returns how many rows merged, or `false` on a day with no
  record, which is the refusal the chip is gated on.

The **indicator** sits under the mode chips: whether the day already has a
schedule, its template, its start, how many blocks are filled, when it was
exported. In `patch` it names the slots about to change; in `blocks` it says out
loud that the day will be rebuilt around what you send.

**The other side of the contract.** The description is parsed by the 22:00 cloud
agent, and that agent aborts on any `mode` it does not recognise — so ROOT's half
alone would have produced a task that fails on the night. `SCHEDULE-AGENT-PROMPT.md`
at the workspace root now carries a `MODE: patch` section: compute every slot
from the calendar's actual `routine p1` (ignore `start:` entirely — ROOT sends
the day's own, so a disagreement means the day really moved), archive-then-write
each **named** slot the `full` way, touch nothing else, and refuse a spec that
names no slots. `mode` validation, the notification line and the never-broken
rule about deleting were widened with it, and §3.4 records the one new trade:
`patch` has no start-time tolerance and reports no discrepancy.

**That file is not code and this commit cannot deploy it.** The routine holds
its own copy of the prompt; it has to be re-pasted there before a `patch` export
will do anything but abort. See *Deferred*.

### CREATE (tasks 1, 2, 8, 9)

- **The count that stuck.** The band is not part of the home screen — the shell
  lifts it out — so the area chip redrew the shelf and left the last chip's
  number on the row. Both the listener and the exported `area()` call `render()`
  now.
- **The tally.** On `all`, one number was three unlike things added together. It
  is one number per chip now, each captioned with the chip it counts, in the
  place the rolling number sat. Curate is a column exactly when curate is a chip.
  Every other filter keeps the single number and its shuffle.
- **The loose session.** The same form the work screen has, on the session log,
  with no work behind it. The store already allowed it (`work` has been nullable
  since the songs → works migration), so this is a form and a row style. The area
  is still asked, and its row says the area instead of a name rather than a dash.
- **Two bars.** The stages of the area, lit up to the one the work is on, then
  the steps of that stage. Rounded squares at `--r1`. A terminal stage has no
  step bar rather than an empty rail; the 16-item rail fallback applies to the
  step bar alone.
- **Six stage palettes** in the editor — `spectrum` `ember` `ocean` `forest`
  `dusk` `mono` — sampled across however many stages an area has rather than
  cycled, so the last stage always lands on the last colour. The area takes the
  head of its own ramp. The swatches stay editable and nothing records which
  palette was used. `data-ed` is the editor's third verb alongside `add`/`del`.

## Verification

`cd root/test && node harness.mjs` — **995 passed, 0 failed**, up from 992 and
from 906/24 mid-build.

Twenty-four existing checks failed on the first run and every one of them was a
behaviour 4.3 deliberately changed: the app count (9 → 10), the shape of `--sat`,
CREATE's band label and its progress ticks, the progress rail's radius, and the
audio stub, which had no `createBiquadFilter` and so silently swallowed every
voice with a lowpass on it. Each was updated to assert the new behaviour rather
than relaxed. One — "CREATE's number shuffles when it changes" — was **rewritten**
rather than updated: it had been passing on a stale animation left over from
three steps earlier rather than on the action it names, and now drives the change
through the store and asserts the number actually moved.

New checks cover: the loose session (written, counted, drawn, and refused with no
hours), the tally on `all` and the single number elsewhere, the `render()` fix,
the two progress bars and their labels and the step-bar-only rail fallback, the
stage palettes (applied, sampled, one area only), the sound kits and the map and
the three-call-sites/zero-in-modules invariant, the four new dials writing whole
pixels onto the root, `CAL.patch()` merging only named slots and refusing an
unknown day, and TOOLS end to end — the strip, the pomodoro reading Config,
starting/pausing storing a timestamp rather than a count, **a phase whose end is
already in the past finishing on the next tick**, the countdown, the stopwatch's
laps, the decider never repeating itself, and search and the storage report
finding the app.

`node peek.mjs` was run to read the new screens as words: the two progress bars
draw with the right segment counts and labels, the band's tally reads
`2 production · 2 mixing · 8 curate`, and all four TOOLS instruments render with
their readouts and controls.

**Not verified:** anything that needs eyes or ears, and anything outside this
repo. jsdom does not lay out or paint, so nothing here proves how the Content
offset looks on the phone, whether the pill's three dials are set to good
defaults for Hugo's hand, whether the new voices are "clicky but subtle" on a
real speaker, or how the ring and the tally sit on a real screen. And nothing
here exercises the 22:00 agent: `mode: patch` is verified only as far as the
description ROOT writes and the record CAL keeps — the calendar half was written
into `SCHEDULE-AGENT-PROMPT.md` and has never run.
