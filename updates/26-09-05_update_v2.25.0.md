## What was done

### The blur — found (task 1)

**`-webkit-overflow-scrolling:touch`.**

That property opts a scroller into iOS's legacy accelerated-scrolling path. On
that path the scroller and anything composited over it are rasterised into
layers and re-scaled rather than redrawn, and text on a re-scaled layer is
resampled — soft edges, worst on the smallest type. `.h-top` sits directly over
`.view-body` at z-index 20. That is why the softness landed on the band and
nowhere else on the page, and why the date line — 10px, letter-spaced, muted —
was the first thing to show it. It is invisible on a desktop browser, which is
why three sessions of reading never reproduced it.

Two releases went looking for a fractional pixel instead: `--title-px` snapped
the wordmark in 2.24.0, `--sat` snapped the status-bar inset in 2.24.1. Both of
those are real defects and both are kept. Neither was this one. **Hugo pasting
the screenshot and saying "it's from the old patch but it's the same display
issue, nothing changed" is what ruled the entire class out** — two independent
fixes to the geometry, no change at all, means the geometry was never it.

All seven uses are gone, across five stylesheets. The property has done nothing
since iOS 13, where momentum scrolling became the default, and it is deprecated.
The sideways strips keep their `touch-action:pan-x pan-y`, which is the rule
that actually makes them draggable under a finger — a check now asserts both
halves, so removing the dead one cannot take the live one with it.

### LOG — the arrows step aside (task 2)

DAY's stepper fades once idle and returns on the first touch anywhere on the
app. LOG's date arrows are the same control doing the same job, so they now do
the same thing and read the **same dial** — `calStepsHide`, not a second one:
"how long before a stepper gets out of the way" is one question and should not
have two answers that can disagree.

They fade rather than being removed. Taking them out of the row would move the
date sideways under them every time they went.

### LOG and DAY — the big date shuffles (tasks 3 and 4)

A vertical roll says "the next one along", which is right for a counter and
wrong for a date. Stepping through days is riffling a deck: the number that
leaves is flicked off to one side, tilted seven degrees and blurred as it goes,
while the next drops in from the other side and settles. The blur is what makes
it a shuffle rather than a slide — a card leaving the hand is not in focus, and
without it the tilt reads as a glitch.

`--dn-dir` carries the direction the date moved, so forward throws the old
number left and back throws it right: the animation and the gesture agree about
which way time went.

It moved into `Shell.dayNum()` rather than being copied into `cal.js`. Task 4
asks for "the same big date as in log", and the only way two things stay the
same is by being one thing (§3) — two copies would be two places to change and
they would drift.

**Two bugs came out of writing it once.** The cleanup timer was module-level, so
whichever box shuffled second cancelled the other's cleanup and left its
outgoing digits in the DOM to pile up behind the live number — the harness
caught it as a `.dn-cur` reading `55652754`. It is per-box state now. And a fast
burst of steps — a finger held on the arrow — appended one outgoing digit per
step, stacking them all against the same right edge until the stepping stopped.
There is exactly one number on its way out now, per box, ever.

### STORE — the list total, and the pinned cost (tasks 5 and 6)

The band gets `.h-band-end`, holding both.

- **The list total**, always: `3/11 items`, in the band's small type, the way
  every other app puts its meta on that line.
- **The trip's cost**, only while the counter is pinned: the big-number
  treatment LOG and DAY use for the date, in the accent, carrying its currency,
  and going red when the widget's budget line does.

They share **one flex end deliberately**. Mounting the cost is what pushes the
item count leftwards — nothing is told to move, so the two cannot get out of
step with each other. That is task 6's "it slides the new items counter to the
left", implemented as a consequence rather than as a second animation.

They are the same information at two scales, so they are drawn as different
kinds of thing — small muted type against the wordmark-sized accent number.
That is task 6's "distinguishly different", and the test for it is whether you
can tell them apart at a glance in a shop.

With the price up in the band the widget stops drawing it (`.cw-total` hidden
while pinned) and keeps everything the band cannot carry: the budget line, the
bar, and the keys. A pinned counter that were only a readout would not be worth
pinning.

And it locks flush. `top:0`, not `top:calc(-1 * var(--bw))` — tucking the widget
a border-width above the scroller's edge hid its own top border at exactly the
moment it locked, so the box looked open at the top for as long as it was
pinned. That is the last clause of task 6.

### More fonts, and a size for the sticky title (task 7)

**Six display faces and three mono**, chosen to widen the *range* rather than
lengthen the list: a condensed grotesque (Oswald), a geometric (Poppins), a slab
(Chivo), a high-contrast serif (Playfair), a rounded face (Nunito) and an
editorial one (DM Serif); Courier Prime, Red Hat Mono and Source Code Pro for
the mono side. Each names the two weights the app actually renders — a face is
not worth offering at a weight the titles cannot draw.

**The sticky sub-screen title gets its own size dial.** `.hd-title` had `15px`
written into six stylesheets as a literal, so the existing Title size dial
reached the eight home wordmarks and nothing else — the header you sit under
while actually working was the one piece of type in the app with no say over it.
`--hd-title-px` is its own five-step dial (`hdTitleSize`), separate from the
wordmark's on purpose: the wordmark is glanced at on the way past, this one is
read at arm's length, and wanting one big and the other small is a real thing to
want.

"Make sure it adjusts the dimensions of its box correctly" is `--hd-pad`, which
is derived from the size rather than being a fixed 14px — so a larger title gets
a proportionally taller header instead of a big word crammed into a small bar.
Both are snapped with `round()`, for the same reason the band is.

**Decision made on Hugo's behalf:** which fonts. The task said "more font
options" without naming any, so they were picked on one rule — every addition
had to bring a shape the list did not already have.
