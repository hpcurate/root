## What was done

### 1. TRACK counts from the day you start

Ticking off what you had already done made the first week read as a sprint and
the projected finish read as a fantasy. `trackFrom` splits the two: ticks dated
before it are **banked** — still done, still counted, drawn as the trend's
starting height — and only what comes after moves the pace.

"Everything ticked is my start" sets the date to **tomorrow** rather than
rewriting any tick's date. That is what makes it safe to press twice and what
keeps the record of when each topic was actually finished. Null falls back to
`startDate`, so an install that never touches it behaves exactly as before.

### 2. The "!" moved to the tab it is about

You were right that it was on the wrong logo. LOG's three rules were all
flagging LOG, including "nothing planned for tomorrow" — a signpost pointing at
the wrong door. That one flags **PLAN** now. LOG still owns all three rules,
the hours, the preview and the only calls to `Shell.alert`; `alertReasons()`
returns *every* rule firing, because they are independent once they no longer
share a tab.

### 3. The "!" wears a filled pill

Like the active tab does — the warning colour on an idle tab, the accent on the
active one. A thin "!" in a bar of thin marks read as just another icon.

### 4 + 2a. Two gaps

`.td-status` has a gap above as well as below (it was butting onto the row of
buttons it reports on). `.set-content` — the content editors that close every
app panel — carries its own top gap, so it never butts onto a danger button
again. Both fixes are general, so they apply in every panel rather than only
the one you spotted.

### 5. Quick cards

`.qk-sub` takes `var(--bk-c)`, the card's own colour, exactly as a block tile's
`.bk-tag` does.

### 6. A third medication slot — and the last one that needs code

The two were named by hand in a dozen places: the form, the record, the export,
the parser, the history pills and both reports. They are `log.meds`'s **keys**
now and everything walks `medKeys()`.

The keys stay the contract — the record writes `meds_<key>`, the `.md` exports
one row per key in Config's order — so adding one is **additive**: a note
written yesterday has no row for it and reads exactly as it always did, because
the parser looks rows up by name. The new slot ships labelled `medication` and
is editable in settings → apps → log like the other two.

### 7. PLAN's border fade

Now 42% of the move's length instead of matching it. The border is the thing
you notice last, so it has to finish first for the gesture to read as fluid
rather than as a box still settling after it has stopped.

### 8. DAY's tab icon

A sun over its rays. The agenda mark it replaced was three bars on a rail,
which at 19px is indistinguishable from PLAN's month grid — and 19px is the
only size that matters here.

### 9 + 10. The date up top, the arrows down below

The date moved into the title band beside the wordmark, right-aligned and
vertically centred, at a **declared 32px height** — anything sharing the
wordmark's row that can grow past it makes this band the odd one out and the
title morph stumble.

The arrows moved out of the slide entirely into a fixed pill above the nav,
sharing the nav's chrome treatment and hiding with it. They *had* to leave the
slide: nothing `position:fixed` may live inside `#track`, since a transformed
ancestor becomes its containing block and the title morph animates one. The
stepper is a sibling of `#views` carrying `.ns-cal`, so cal.js's one delegated
listener still reaches it, and CSS shows it only while DAY's slide is current.

### 11. A blur setting

Settings → appearance → layout → "Blur behind the bar". It is the most
expensive thing the chrome does, and on a tired phone it is what makes the bar
smear as the page moves.

### 12. The empty day

"open plan" is one of DAY's own controls now — a compact accent pill — rather
than a `.btn` stretched across a card, which read as a screen borrowed from a
different app.
