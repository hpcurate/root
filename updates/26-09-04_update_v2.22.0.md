## What was done

### The app asks its own questions

`window.confirm` and `window.prompt` are gone from ROOT — all 28 confirms and 4
prompts. One `#ask` overlay (`index.html`, `shell.css`, `Shell.ask` in
`shell.js`) does both, built from the same tokens as everything else. It carries
the `.modal-overlay` and `.modal-cancel` class names deliberately: that is how
the shell already finds overlays, so Escape closing it and the keyboard
shortcuts stepping aside came for free rather than as new wiring.

**This changed a calling convention, which is the part to know about.** An
overlay's answer arrives on a tap, not on the call, so
`if (!Shell.confirm(…)) return;` cannot work any more. Every call site is now
`Shell.confirm(msg, () => { … })`; the five that were already `async` (DO's
defer, LEARN's four) take `await Shell.confirm(msg)` and read almost exactly as
they did. Getting it wrong is *silent* — the guard is falsy, the function
returns, nothing happens — so ROOT.md §6 says so twice and two harness checks
enforce it: one greps every module for a bare `confirm(`, one counts anything
that reaches `window.confirm` across the entire run.

**A decision made on your behalf:** the questions split in two on the way. Most
are destructive and route through `Shell.confirm`, which honours "confirm before
clearing". Four are *not* about clearing and must be asked whatever that setting
says — LOG's "go back without saving?", LOG's import, the data panel's restore,
PLAN's "no project mapped, add anyway?" — and they call `Shell.ask` directly.
They were already the odd ones out in the old code; this makes it explicit
rather than accidental.

`prompt()` was in scope by implication rather than by name: your bullet said
confirmation screens, but a `prompt()` box is the same system dialog and would
have left four of them behind in an app whose stated goal is none. The four
(LEARN's deck rename, PLAN's preset name, TRACK's date, settings' paste-a-look)
use the same overlay with a field in it.

### The numpad

`shell.js` grew a "The numpad" section: one pad for every numeric field in every
app and in settings. It claims any `input` that is `type=number`,
`inputmode=numeric` or `inputmode=decimal`, and nothing else — a text field
still gets the system keyboard, which is the distinction you asked for.

The keyboard is suppressed with `inputmode="none"`, set on **pointerdown**
rather than on focus, because pointerdown is the only moment early enough to
beat the keyboard coming up. The field's own inputmode is remembered in
`data-pad-im`, so switching the pad off in settings hands every field back
without a reload.

Four readings, two inferred and two declared in the markup with `data-pad`:

| kind | typed | means | stored |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `int` | `65` | 65 | `65` |
| `decimal` | `3.5` | 3.5 | `3.5` |
| `duration` | `720` | 7h20m | `7.33` |
| `clock` | `930` | 09:30 | `09:30` |

`duration` is on sleep hours; `clock` is on the three alert-hour fields, which
were `inputmode="numeric"` holding `"10:00"` and would have been misread as
plain integers. The pad shows both readings at once — `7h20m` big, `saved as
7.33` under it — so the conversion is visible rather than trusted. Nonsense is
refused rather than written: `9309` is not a time, and it says so instead of
storing something wrong.

**A decision made on your behalf:** a new behaviour dial, `numpad`, with
`auto` / `always` / `never`, defaulting to `auto` — the pad appears on a touch
screen and stays out of the way on a laptop, where the system keyboard is a real
keyboard and was never the problem. `always` is there if you disagree.

Second one: the first digit after the pad opens **replaces** the field's value
rather than appending to it, the way typing over a selected field does. Without
it, tapping a filled clock field and typing was capped at four digits and did
nothing at all.

### LOG's home, on one screen

The eight cards moved down and got smaller; the two full-width utility cards now
share a row. The space that freed up holds two new things, both read straight
out of the day records — nothing new is stored and nothing is derived that the
reports do not already derive:

- **A month of days**, each cell drawn by how much of that day was written
  (neither half, one, both). Today is outlined, the selected day is ringed,
  tomorrow is not offered. `‹ ›` walk the months and stop at the current one.
- **A fortnight of energy and mood**, two lines over one box. Not three: three
  lines in 48px is a texture, not a reading — stress stays in the reports where
  there is room to say what it means. A gap in the data is drawn as a gap, never
  as a line straight through it.

A cell is also a control: tapping one selects that day, which is the same
selection the band's two arrows move one step at a time. A day three weeks ago
used to be twenty taps away.

"No scrolling" is enforced by making the layout height-driven rather than
content-driven: `#s-home` is a flex column at `height:100%` with `overflow:hidden`,
the calendar takes the slack, the cards are fixed. **A decision made on your
behalf:** this only applies above 560px of viewport height. Below that — a
laptop window, a phone in landscape with the curtain off — it goes back to
flowing and scrolling, because "one screen" is a promise about a phone held
upright and not a reason to clip content everywhere else.

### DAY

- **Opens on today, always.** `pickDefault()` returned the nearest day that had
  something on it, so a morning with nothing planned answered a question nobody
  asked and you had to step *back* to learn today was empty.
- **The stepper spans the screen.** It was an 80px pill floating over the middle
  of the day — the one place neither thumb naturally lands, and the one place a
  mis-tap costs you what you were reading. Each arrow is half the width now, at
  40px tall with 22px chevrons. Interpreted as you noted: the two hit areas
  together span the screen; the glyphs are not stretched.
- **It steps aside when idle.** Being that wide it covers the bottom of the day,
  so it fades after five seconds and returns on the first touch anywhere on DAY.
  The five seconds is a dial (`calStepsHide`, 0 pins it) rather than a literal,
  which is how every other number in ROOT is expressed.
- **OPEN PLAN in upper case.** Written into ROOT.md §9 as a deliberate exception
  to DAY's own lower-case rule, with the reason: it is the only action on that
  screen, and the only thing there that has to be found rather than read.
- **A day can clear itself**, from a small control in its head, behind a
  confirmation that says the Todoist task stays where it is — withdrawing that
  is not ROOT's to do.

### LOG's medication slots

`m3` ships **off**. The mechanism is a new `log.medsOn` branch, a fixed set of
booleans read through the shipped defaults — the same pattern `plan.formFields`
uses and safe for the same reason. It hides the slot **from the form and from
nothing else**: the record keeps what it has, the `.md` still exports a row per
key, and turning it back on shows yesterday's answer where it was. That is the
rule ROOT.md already states for `log.fields`, and filtering the export by it
would have broken the Obsidian contract you asked me not to touch.

The colour is `log.medColors`, written to `--med-c` by `log.js`. The two
hardcoded selectors (`.med-b.on.lam`, `.med-b.on.rit`) became **one** rule, so
`m3` is highlighted like the others and a fourth slot would be too, without a
line of CSS. `m3` is green, distinct from lamotrigine's blue and ritalin's
orange.

### DO's quick cards

The card is now drawn in its label's colour the way a block tile is — a tinted
ground and a coloured border, not a coloured line inside a grey box. **The
colour comes from the *other* label**: `@quick` is the filter, so it is on every
one of these by definition and colouring by it says nothing. `fetchQuick()`
builds a name→hex map from the labels call it already makes, picks the first
label on the task that is not `@quick`, and the second line names that label
rather than always saying `@quick`. A card with no second label keeps `@quick`'s
own colour.

**Clearing what is finished:** a ticked quick card stays on the list until
midnight, which is the only way unticking it can exist (Todoist never returns a
closed task again). Right for the minute after you tick it, wrong for the four
hours after that. `clear N` drops them early — local only, nothing is reopened,
and a refresh does not bring them back because Todoist has them closed.

### PLAN's border fade

`.42` → `.22` of the move (≈150ms against 680ms), floored at 70ms. 2.21 took it
from 100% to 42% and it still was not fluid, because at 286ms the border was
still visibly resolving after the box had landed — by the time the box arrives
there has to be nothing left to watch. The tile's own CSS transition went
`.14s` → `.12s` too: it was about to become the slow one.

### STORE's counter

A pin on the widget itself. Pinned, it sticks to the top of the page while you
work down the list, and the ± rows fold away so it costs two lines rather than
half the screen. **sticky, not fixed** — `position:fixed` inside `#track` takes
a transformed ancestor as its containing block, which ROOT.md flags as having
caused two bugs already. The state lives in `store_state_v1`, so it survives a
reload. New `#ico-pin` sprite symbol; the harness's duplicate-icon check passes.

## Deferred

Nothing from the request. Two things noted rather than done:

- **The numpad is not offered on `type="time"` fields** (LOG's wake time). Those
  get the platform's own time picker, which is a purpose-built control rather
  than a keyboard, so it is not the thing the request was about.
- **`log.medColors` has no editor.** The colours are Config data now rather than
  CSS literals, so one is a small addition later; nothing in the request asked
  to choose them.
